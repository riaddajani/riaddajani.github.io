Overview

Superpower Social is an AI platform that hides toxic comments and drafts on-brand replies for brands running paid-social ads. Brands and agencies connect their Meta ad accounts or social pages, train the AI on their moderation guidelines and brand voice, and scale to 24/7 automated moderation and response generation.

The product solves a high-friction problem: brands running Facebook and Instagram ads cannot keep up with the volume of comments—spam, hate, and genuine engagement alike—without sacrificing authenticity or burning hours on manual moderation.

As a founder, I worked directly with early users to understand how they decide when and how to respond. These insights shaped the system from the ground up—turning subjective engagement decisions into measurable signals and production-ready pipelines.

The result is a real-time interaction engine that combines lightweight predictive modeling with controlled LLM generation to deliver scalable, high-quality responses.

Origin

Right after graduating from my Master's, I moved to Toronto and started building Superpower Social out of my co-founder's basement.

This was my entire setup—workspace, living space, and everything in between.

I operated out of this environment while designing, building, and deploying the first versions of the system, working end-to-end across modeling, infrastructure, and product iteration.

The constraint wasn't just technical—it was personal. Every decision had to balance speed, cost, and real user impact. There was no separation between "work" and "life"—the system was built in continuous cycles of building, testing, and refining.

This experience shaped how I think about systems:

  • Prioritize what actually moves the needle
  • Ship fast, observe real behavior, and adapt
  • Build for real constraints, not ideal ones

Superpower Social is the result of that process.

What I Learned

Most of what I learned building Superpower came from getting things wrong first. We over-engineered early versions—adding features "just in case," optimizing systems that shouldn't have existed yet, and building for scale before we had users. The biggest shift was learning to question every requirement ruthlessly, especially the ones that felt obvious. If something couldn't be justified by real user behavior, it got cut.

The second thing was that nothing replaces direct contact with users. Early on, we onboarded brands manually—walking them through every step, watching how they actually used the product, and building around their real workflows instead of our assumptions. That unscalable, hands-on work was the highest-leverage thing we did. It taught us what mattered, and more importantly, what didn't.

I also learned that speed is only valuable when you're pointed in the right direction. We wasted weeks accelerating features that users never asked for. The discipline became: first, make sure the problem is real. Then, strip the solution down to the smallest thing that works. Only then do you move fast—and only then do you automate. Automating a broken process just scales the dysfunction.

The last lesson was about commitment. There were stretches where nothing worked—users churned, the pipeline broke, money was tight. What kept the company alive wasn't a clever strategy, it was the refusal to stop. Startups don't die from competition—they die from founders giving up. Every problem we faced had a solution on the other side of persistence.

From Idea to Production System

The system was built iteratively in tight feedback loops with users:

  • Early versions overused LLMs — too slow and expensive
  • Users needed control + authenticity, not just automation
  • "Good response" had to be learned per creator, not globally defined

These constraints led to a shift from a purely generative system to a decision-first architecture, where generation is only used when it adds value.

System Architecture: Real-Time Decision + Generation Pipeline

Superpower uses a two-stage production pipeline designed around real-world constraints (latency, cost, and user trust):

1. Real-Time Decision Layer (≤5ms inference)

Used for both reply prioritization and comment moderation. The key insight was treating every user interaction in the UI—replying, skipping, regenerating a response, hiding, deleting—as implicit signal. The model learns each creator's behavior, preferences, and patterns in their comment management workflow through these actions.

  • Per-creator logistic regression model trained on 90-day interaction history
  • Online updates via SGD after each user action (reply, skip, regenerate, hide, delete)
  • MiniLM embedding features + behavioral signals from UI interactions
  • Outputs probability of engagement and moderation decisions in real time

2. Controlled Generation Layer (LLM Routing)

  • Only high-value interactions are escalated
  • Responses generated via structured, self-improving prompt systems
  • Prompts iterated based on user feedback and observed performance

Reduced LLM usage by ~83% while improving response relevance and maintaining brand voice.

Why It Matters
